3rd Grade Ukulele — 2026-2027

Every third grader takes ukulele. Here's how it works — it's simpler than band, and there's nothing to buy.

The Short Version

Nothing to buy, nothing to sign

Ukulele is part of the regular third grade day for every student, and class meets Tuesday and Thursday. It doesn't work like band: there's no instrument to rent, no book to purchase, no private lessons to arrange, and no participation agreement to sign.

The instrument

Provided. Your student uses a school ukulele in class and may take it home midweek — see the schedule below. Bring it to class every Tuesday and Thursday.

The materials

No book to buy. Everything students need is in a presentation used in class — and I email that presentation and the practice material home, so your student can work on the same thing at home.

Taking It Home

Tuesday and Wednesday only

Ukuleles may go home on Tuesday and Wednesday nights so students can practice. Every other night they stay at school.

Ukuleles need to be back at school Thursday, and they don't go home for the weekend. Instruments are shared and needed for other classes, and weekends are where they get lost or damaged.

Over a school break

Breaks are the exception — your student may keep the ukulele over a long weekend or holiday to practice. The one thing I ask is that you email me before the instrument leaves the building, so I know where every ukulele is while school is out.

Practice

Expected, but on the honor system

Students are expected to practice enough to keep up with the class. There's no log to fill out and no app to track — unlike band, ukulele practice runs on the honor system.

What "keeping up" looks like

A few minutes on the days the ukulele comes home is plenty at this age. I email the class presentation and practice material home, so your student is working on exactly what we did in class. If your student is falling behind, you'll hear from me before it becomes a problem.

Tuning

You don't need to tune it

Ukuleles don't need to be tuned before class — in class we focus on learning correct fingerings, and tuning won't hold your student back.

If you know how

Parents who know how to tune a ukulele are welcome to. It's recommended, though not required, that your student practices on a tuned instrument at home.

If you don't

Completely fine. I'll email a free tuning app you can use if you'd like to — it takes about a minute and no musical experience.

What to expect

Students perform three songs at each concert, and all music is memorized — no sheet music on stage. That's very achievable at this age with the practice material sent home, and it's a real accomplishment for a third grader.

Bringing the Instrument to Class

What happens if it's left at home

Your student needs their ukulele in class on the days it's been taken home. When it's missing they can't play, so this matters more than it might sound.

  1. First day without it

    A clip down, handled in class. Nothing comes home.

  2. Two days in a row

    You'll get a note from me in Veracross, so it's on your radar early.

  3. Three days in a row

    We'll have a real conversation — you, me, and your student — about what's getting in the way. Something is usually going on, and it's easier to fix once we know what it is.

Common Questions

A few things parents ask

Do we sign the band agreement?

No. That agreement is for band students who rent an instrument. Ukulele families don't need to sign anything.

Should we buy our own ukulele?

You don't need to. If your student loves it and you want one at home, that's great — but it isn't expected or required.

Does ukulele lead into band?

It's a strong start. Students learn to read music, keep time, and use both hands independently — all of which transfers directly to a band instrument in 4th grade.

Can my student bring a pick or a strap?

Both are fine in class if your student wants them. Picks aren't used during concerts, though — so it's worth practicing without one too.

What if the ukulele gets damaged?

Students are expected to take care of their instrument, and a damaged ukulele needs to be replaced by the family. Tell me as soon as something happens — normal wear is one thing, and I'd much rather sort it out early than find it in class.
