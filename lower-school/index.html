The First Three Weeks

How the year starts

Your student does not need to have anything in hand yet. Here is exactly how it goes, day by day.

  1. Tuesday, August 11

    Combined music class in the theater, and instrument assignments go out this day. Section seats are limited, so not every student gets their first choice.

  2. Thursday, August 13

    Band and chorus meet separately for the first time. First day of band, in the band room. If your student's instrument has not arrived yet, that is completely fine. This class is mostly classroom routines and band expectations.

  3. Tuesday, August 18

    Have the instrument by this day. If you rent through Joyful Noise, they can deliver it straight to school by no later than Tuesday the 18th. Start the rental as soon as you have the assignment on the 11th.

  4. Tuesday, August 25

    Everything starts counting here. Instrument and book expected every class, and practice logging begins. Nothing before this date counts against your student.

Band is a full-year commitment. A section losing a player affects everyone in it, so students don't drop partway through except for a medical reason or special permission from administration. If band starts to feel impossible, email me before it gets to that point.

Strings

Violin and cello are welcome

Your student may play violin or cello in band. I don't teach those instruments, so a student on one needs their own teacher, but if they already play, they have a real part in the group.

Which book

Violin uses the oboe edition of Essential Elements for Band, Book 1. Cello uses the trombone edition. Those are the parts that line up with what the band is playing.

The scales are different

The scales taught to beginning strings usually aren't the ones beginning band learns. Your student will need to learn the scales we cover in class, not the ones from a strings method book.

The Book

Provided, and yours to buy if you'd rather

4th and 5th grade use Essential Elements for Band, Book 1. A book is provided for every student, so there is nothing you have to purchase.

Buying your own is optional

Some families prefer their student have a copy that stays home and can be written in. If that's you, it's about $15 from Amazon or any music retailer. Get the edition for your student's specific instrument. A trumpet book won't work for a clarinet.

The One Thing That Matters Most

Practice 90 minutes a week, at home

Class time alone isn't enough to make progress. Every band student practices at home, 90 minutes a week. Three sessions of 30 minutes is what I recommend, but how your student splits it is up to you.

Practice logs start Tuesday, August 25. Nothing needs to be logged before then. Use the first two weeks to get the instrument home and get set up, and start logging on the 25th.

What counts as practice

Yes: your student playing on their own, at home.

No: band class on Tuesday and Thursday. That is rehearsal, not practice.

No: time in a private lesson. That is instruction, not practice.

Practicing after a lesson, working on what the teacher assigned, absolutely counts. Sitting in the lesson does not.

How a practice week gets counted

A week runs Monday through Sunday. Log by Sunday night.

90 minutes a week, split however you like. Three sessions of 30 minutes is what I recommend, but two longer sessions or seven short ones count exactly the same. The weekly total is the standard.

MakeMusic logs it for you. Nothing to submit and nothing to turn in. It only counts time spent inside the app, so keep it open even on short nights.

The practice log form submits one day at a time. Practice Monday, submit Monday, done. No waiting until you have three sessions to report.

Mixing is fine. App some nights, form others. Both land in the same place.

A PDF still counts, but please go digital if you can. MakeMusic and the form are the only two versions I can see without entering anything by hand. If neither will work, the PDF is linked right under the gold button above: fill it in and email it to me. Printing it and sending in a physical sheet is the very last resort.

I review logs weekly. If 10 days pass with nothing logged, you will get a short email from me. That is a heads-up, not a warning.

MakeMusic Cloud is completely optional. Many families have thoughtful policies around screen time, and I respect that. Either digital option works and your student is at no disadvantage, because the practice standard is the same for everyone.

How Band Is Graded

Half the grade is showing up ready. Half is practicing.

There are no surprises in a band grade and no judgment about talent. Your student is graded on two things they fully control, and both are already being tracked, so nothing here asks anything new of you.

Both halves start Tuesday, August 25. Instrument and book are expected in class from that day, and practice logging begins the same day. The first two weeks are for getting set up and nothing in them counts against your student.

50% — Ready for class

Three things, every class: on time, instrument in hand, book in hand. One point each. Band meets Tuesday and Thursday, so that is 8 classes and 24 points in a grading cycle.

50% — Practice

Ninety minutes of practice a week, split however your student likes. Three sessions of 30 minutes is what I recommend. Over a four-week cycle that is six hours, logged automatically in MakeMusic Cloud, or one submission per session on the practice log form.

Why half and half. A student who never misses a class but never opens the case at home earns exactly half the points. A student who practices faithfully but leaves the instrument at home earns the same. Neither half can carry the grade, and that is deliberate. Band is the one subject where the work between classes is the subject.

What Lands On The Report Card

4th and 5th grade band is not a letter grade. It stays an S / U / E mark, exactly as it has been. The points above are what decide which mark your student earns.

S — the normal mark

Putting in the effort and practicing. This is most of the class, and it is a good mark, not a warning.

E — going beyond

Consistently prepared and practicing well past the minimum, cycle after cycle.

U — not yet

Reserved for a student who is regularly unprepared and not practicing. You will have heard from me well before this ever appears on a report card.

The Grading Cycle

Marks are figured every four weeks, two per quarter, so the first reads like a midterm and the second like a final. You will never be surprised in October by something that started going wrong in August.

Concerts And The Grade

The concert is not graded

For 4th and 5th grade, there is no grade penalty for missing a concert. The two halves above are the whole mark, in every quarter, concert or no concert. I want your student on that stage because it is the best night of the year, not because a grade is holding them there.

One request: if your student is sick, please keep them home. Nothing about the grade depends on being there, so there is never a reason to bring a sick child to a concert. A quick note to let me know is all I need.

Life Happens

These are written down on purpose, so you never have to wonder and I never have to decide in the moment.

  • Excused absence. Those points come out of the total, not off the grade. Missing class for a real reason cannot hurt your student.
  • Instrument in the shop. Let me know ahead of time and both preparedness and practice are excused for those days.
  • Borrowing a classmate's book counts as not having one. The habit is what is being graded, not the object.
  • MakeMusic is down. A handwritten log is accepted that week, no penalty.
  • Joining band mid-cycle. Grading starts at your student's first class, not before it.
  • Two unprepared days in a row gets you a note from me in Veracross the same day. Three in a row and I call you. I would rather have one real conversation than send you three notices.
You will hear from me early, not late. Every one of these numbers is visible to me the day it happens, which means you find out while there is still time to fix it.
