Cost

What does band actually cost per month?

It depends on the instrument and how often your student takes lessons. There's an investment calculator on the Lower School band page that gives you a real number. Pick the instrument, set the lesson rate and frequency, and it shows the monthly cost and the full-year total. At the low end, most instruments run about $130 a month with weekly lessons, and lessons are the large majority of that. The instrument rental itself is $30 a month for most instruments.

Can we buy an instrument instead of renting?

You can. Joyful Noise applies 50% of what you've already paid in rental fees toward buying a used instrument later, so renting first doesn't waste money if you decide to purchase. Talk to them directly at 919-386-9335. My advice is to rent for the first year. Beginning students change instruments more often than parents expect, and a rental keeps that from being an expensive mistake.

What if we can't afford the rental or lessons?

Talk to me before you count band out, because there are usually more options than families realize. Start with your own circles. Instruments sit unused in a lot of houses, and someone at your church or in your neighborhood may be willing to lend one or pass it along, which takes care of the largest recurring cost. Lessons can sometimes work the same way, where a friend or a musician at church teaches your student at no charge. Be realistic about that one: those lessons tend to be occasional rather than weekly, so treat it as a real help rather than a full replacement for a regular teacher. If the money genuinely can't go toward band this year, choir is the best alternative. There's no instrument to rent, no book to buy, and no lesson cost. Mrs. Maloney is an outstanding teacher, and your student will learn to love music in her class. Students who start in choir often move into band in a later year, so the door stays open. Either way, email me and we'll find the right fit for your student.

Can my student drop band partway through the year?

No, not by choice. Band runs the full year. The group depends on every part being there, and a section losing a player mid-year affects everyone else in it. The exceptions are a medical reason or special permission from administration. If something is going on that makes band feel impossible, email me before it gets to that point; most of the time there's a fix that doesn't involve leaving.

Should we keep the rental over the summer?

That's your call and you arrange it directly with Joyful Noise. They send a return-and-summer-rental notice near the end of the year. Keeping it means your student can play over the summer, which makes September much easier. Returning it is fine too. If your student is thinking about switching instruments, tell me before you decide, so the switch and the rental line up.

Equipment

Do I need to buy the book, and which one?

No. A book is provided for every student, so there is nothing you have to purchase. 4th and 5th grade use Essential Elements for Band, Book 1; middle and high school use Book 2. If you'd rather your student have their own copy to keep at home and write in, it runs about $15 on Amazon or from any music retailer. Get the edition for your student's specific instrument; a trumpet book won't work for a clarinet.

My student plays percussion. What do we buy, and how loud is it at home?

The school owns the drums, so there is nothing to rent. Snare sticks are the only requirement, about $13. For home practice, a practice pad with a stand runs about $30, is quiet enough for a bedroom, and is the best value of the options for building technique. A full beginner snare kit is around $100 and is genuinely loud; the mute pad it comes with helps. The pad is what I'd recommend for most families.

Does the bass guitar need an amplifier?

Yes, a bass guitar needs an amp to be heard properly. The beginner starter kits include a small practice amp, so if you buy one of those you're covered and there's nothing extra to purchase. If noise at home is a concern, ask me before you buy and I'll point you at an amp with a headphone jack so your student can practice silently.

What happens if my student's instrument breaks or needs repair?

Contact Joyful Noise directly. Repairs and maintenance are included in the rental. They keep a small number of loaner instruments in the band room, so your student can use a loaner and keep playing while theirs is being fixed.

What does a percussion student need to start?

A practice pad and a pair of snare sticks. That's it to begin with, because the school owns the drums and the bells, so there's nothing to rent. A pad and sticks is what I tell every new percussion family.

Instrument Choice

My student has braces. Which instruments still work?

Braces don't rule out band. Flute, clarinet, and saxophone are usually the easiest adjustment. Trumpet and trombone press a mouthpiece directly against the lips, so the first couple of weeks after braces go on, or come off, take some getting used to; plenty of students play brass with braces, and orthodontic wax helps a lot. Percussion is unaffected. If your student has braces or is getting them soon, tell me before instrument choices are made and I'll factor it in.

When does band start, and when do 4th and 5th graders get their instrument?

August 6 and August 11 are combined music classes in the theater, band and chorus together. Instrument assignments go out on August 11. On August 13 the class splits into band and chorus for the first time. That is the first day of band in the band room, and every 4th and 5th grader comes in with their instrument and their book. Section seats are limited, so not every student gets their first choice. Middle and high school band is different: it starts Thursday, August 6, and those students play that day.

We already own an instrument. Can my student use it?

Yes, bring it on Thursday, August 13, the first day of band. There's nothing to rent and nothing to arrange. Just tell me ahead of time what your student has so I can plan the section.

My student takes piano. Which instrument should they consider?

Percussion. Students who already read music and have keyboard experience pick up the bells and the reading side of percussion much faster than students starting from zero, and percussion needs exactly that skill set. It's a long-standing recommendation here and usually the best fit for a piano student joining band.

Lessons

Are private lessons required?

Only for 4th and 5th grade band. They're strongly recommended for middle and high school, but not required.

Where does the private teacher list come from? Are the teachers screened?

The list is maintained by Music & Arts, the music retailer, so these are working professional instructors the store knows and refers, not names pulled off the internet. That said, private lessons are an arrangement between your family and the teacher, not a school program: CCS doesn't employ these instructors and doesn't run background checks on them. Do the same due diligence you would for any private tutor.

Can you recommend a private teacher for my student's instrument?

The Private Teacher List is linked in the Lower School section of this site, and the link is below. Every teacher on it has been vetted, and I recommend anyone on that list, so pick whoever fits your family's schedule and budget. It's organized by instrument with a phone number and email for each teacher. For percussion in 4th and 5th grade, John Myers teaches privately here at CCS and you can contact him directly at jmyers@carychristianschool.org.

When should we set lessons up?

As soon as possible. Don't wait on this, because every week without a teacher slows your student's progress. I don't recommend waiting until September if you can avoid it.

Our private teacher isn't showing up consistently. What should we do?

Switch. There's a full list of private teachers to choose from linked on the band pages. Pick another one from it. You're not locked in to the first teacher you tried, and a teacher who misses weeks isn't going to get your student where they want to be.

How often should my student take lessons?

Weekly is the recommendation. Local teachers generally run $50 to $80 an hour, depending on the teacher. In lower school lessons are typically 30 minutes, so figure roughly $25 to $40 per lesson; middle and high school lessons usually run 45 to 60 minutes at the same hourly rate. Lessons are required for 4th and 5th grade, and strongly recommended for middle and high school. A student taking them weekly progresses noticeably faster than one who doesn't, and the students who stay in band through middle and high school are almost always the ones who had a private teacher early.

Practice

How much should my student practice?

Three times a week, 30 minutes per session, minimum, every grade, no exceptions. Class time alone isn't enough to make progress. This is the single thing that most separates students who thrive in band from students who struggle.

How is practice tracked, and what happens if my student falls behind?

Practice is logged in MakeMusic Cloud. If you'd rather not use the app, practice is tracked through a weekly Google Form instead: one submission a week with each session, the date and time, how long, and what your student worked on. Either way the standard is the same. If ten or more days pass with nothing logged, you'll get a short email from me. That's a heads-up, not a warning, and there's no classroom penalty attached to it. The practice commitment is between you and your student at home; my job is to make sure it isn't quietly slipping.

How does MakeMusic Cloud work, and how do we get set up?

Join with the class code link for your student's group, then follow the steps to create the account. 4th Grade: admin.makemusic.com/join?classCode=M2XEP-L3V3Q. 5th Grade: admin.makemusic.com/join?classCode=Q4XFH-2Y3EU. Middle School: admin.makemusic.com/join?classCode=LG4K3-3VCWF. High School: admin.makemusic.com/join?classCode=CVZDU-ZYZF7. Once you're in, your student practices inside the app and it logs the time on its own, so there's nothing to fill out. It listens while they play and gives instant feedback on what they got right and what needs work, and it emails you a weekly progress report. I can assign the exact music we're working on in class, down to specific passages for individual students. If your student's name isn't showing up in the class list, email me and I'll add them.

Is MakeMusic Cloud required, or optional?

Optional. I know many families have thoughtful policies around screen time and I respect that, and your student is at no disadvantage either way. The practice standard is the same for everyone: three times a week, minimum 30 minutes. MakeMusic is just the easiest way to track it, and it gives your student feedback that a log sheet can't. If you'd rather not use it, there's a weekly practice log form you can submit by hand instead. For middle and high school I'd push harder toward MakeMusic, because at that level the app is doing real teaching between rehearsals.

Practicing at home is loud. Can my student use a mute?

At home, yes. A mute is worth trying if noise is a problem. Ask Joyful Noise or any music retailer. I don't use them in class though: the band room's acoustics make everything loud regardless, and mutes distort the sound enough to be a distraction while we're rehearsing.

In Class

What if my student forgets their instrument?

Your student needs their instrument every class day. The first day without it is a clip down, handled in class. Two days in a row and you'll get a note from me in Veracross, so it's on your radar early. Three in a row means a real conversation with you, me, and your student about what's getting in the way. This is the same in band and ukulele. It isn't a punishment. It's so a pattern doesn't go unnoticed.

What's the difference between general music and band?

General music is about music literacy. Reading, theory, singing, and music history, and every student in the grade takes it. Band is about playing: your student on an instrument, in an ensemble, working toward a performance. They support each other. The reading your student does in general music is exactly what makes the notes on a band page mean something.

There's a makeup assignment, a short playing assessment instead of the performance. Email me and I'll send your student what to prepare. It's usually a scale page or a passage from the back of their book.

My student has a conflict with a dress rehearsal. Can they still perform?

Yes. Email me as soon as you know. Students who miss a dress rehearsal for a legitimate conflict still perform in the concert. I just need to know ahead of time so I can plan around it.

Are there auditions for band?

Not to get in. No student auditions for a spot in band. Students do play for me within their section for chair placement and part assignment, so the parts land with the students ready for them. It's low pressure, it happens during class, and it isn't something to prepare for weeks in advance.

A few times a year students play a short assessment for a grade, usually four scales from the back of the Essential Elements book (Concert B-flat, E-flat, F and A-flat), or a short snare solo for percussionists. Ukulele

Does my 3rd grader need to buy a ukulele or a book?

Neither. The ukulele is provided, and there's no book to buy. Everything students need to learn is in a presentation I use in class. There is nothing to purchase for ukulele. Full details are on the Ukulele page.

Can the ukulele come home?

Yes, on Tuesday and Wednesday nights. Every other night the ukulele stays at school. Instruments are shared, and weekends are where they get lost or damaged.

Is ukulele practice tracked like band practice?

No. Students are expected to practice enough to keep up with the class, but it runs on the honor system, with no log and no app. A few minutes on the days the ukulele comes home is plenty at this age.

Do ukulele families sign the band participation agreement?

No. That agreement is for band students who rent an instrument through Joyful Noise. Ukulele families have nothing to sign.

Can my 3rd grader take the ukulele home over a school break?

Yes. The one thing I ask is that you email me before the instrument leaves the building for a break, so I know where every ukulele is over a long weekend or holiday.

Can my student use their own ukulele instead of a school one?

Yes, that's fine. If you have one at home your student is welcome to use it, and it's one fewer school instrument in circulation. Same rules apply: bring it every Tuesday and Thursday, and take care of it. Let me know so I'm not looking for a school ukulele that was never checked out.

What if my 3rd grader's ukulele gets damaged?

Students are expected to take care of their instrument, and a damaged ukulele needs to be replaced by the family. Tell me as soon as something happens. Normal wear is one thing, and it's much easier to sort out early than to discover it in class.

Does the ukulele need to be tuned before class?

No. In class we focus on correct fingerings and tuning won't hold your student back. If you know how to tune it, you're welcome to, and practicing on a tuned instrument at home is recommended but not required. If you'd like to try, download Ukulele Tuner, a free iPhone app. It takes a minute and no musical experience.

Does 3rd grade ukulele perform in concerts?

Yes, twice: a Christmas concert and a Grandparents Day concert, both separate from the Upper School band concerts. Students perform three songs at each, and all the music is memorized, so there's no sheet music on stage. The Christmas Concert is Wednesday, December 16, 2026 from 9:00 to 10:00am, with a rehearsal Monday, December 14. Grandparents Day is Friday, April 23, 2027, and the time is still to be confirmed.

How does my 3rd grader practice at home without a book?

I email the class presentation and the practice material home, so your student practices exactly what we covered in class. There's no book to buy and nothing to track down.

Communication

How will I know how my student is doing?

Two ways. I post an update in Veracross every two weeks covering what we're working on and what's coming up. Grades are reflected quarterly. Between those, if your student is struggling, you'll hear from me directly; I'm not going to let you find out from a report card. Any email you send gets a reply within 24 hours.

How is band graded?

It depends on the grade. 4th and 5th grade band isn't a letter grade, it's an S / U / E mark on the report card. By default a student earns an S if they're putting in the effort and practicing, which is most of the class. An E is for students going clearly beyond that. Middle and high school band does earn a letter grade every quarter, built from participation, a playing quiz if I give one that quarter, and a performance grade. Alongside the mark there are periodic playing assessments: scale quizzes from the Essential Elements book, and a short snare solo for percussionists, so the grade reflects real playing, not just attendance.

What's the difference between an S and an E on the report card?

S and E marks are 4th and 5th grade only. An S means your student is doing what band asks: coming prepared, putting in the effort, practicing. That's most students and it's a good mark. An E is for students going well beyond that. An S is not a warning sign.
