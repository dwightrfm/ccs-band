Upper School Band 2026-2027

Middle and high school. A performing ensemble, not a beginning class.

Starts Thursday, August 6

We play the first day

Upper School band starts Thursday, August 6, the first week of school and earlier than 4th and 5th grade band. Bring your instrument that day. We're not spending the first class handing things out; we're playing.

Come with your instrument chosen

Students should already know what they're playing and have their instrument in hand. Instrument selection isn't part of the first week the way it is in 4th grade.

Bring it August 6

If you rent through Joyful Noise, arrange it before then so the instrument is in your student's hands on day one.

Who This Class Is For

Experience required

Upper School band moves at the pace of a performing group. Students need enough playing behind them to keep up with the music, not just enthusiasm.

Middle School Band

At least 1 to 2 years of playing experience on the instrument. A student coming out of 5th grade band is well positioned.

High School Band

No new players. The music is too advanced to start from zero at this level. Students joining should already be playing.

Not sure whether your student is ready? Email me before the year starts rather than after. If middle school band isn't the right fit yet, there are better paths in, and it's a much easier conversation in July than in September.

Strings

Violin and cello are welcome

Violin and cello students have a place in Upper School band. I don't teach those instruments, so a student on one needs their own teacher, but if they already play, they have a real part in the group.

Which book

Violin uses the oboe edition of Essential Elements for Band, Book 2. Cello uses the trombone edition. Those are the parts that line up with what the band is playing.

The scales are different

The scales taught to strings players usually aren't the ones we run in band. Your student will need to learn the scales we cover in class, not the ones from a strings method book.

The Book

Provided, and yours to buy if you'd rather

Middle and high school use Essential Elements for Band, Book 2, a step up from the Book 1 that 4th and 5th grade play out of. A book is provided for every student, so there is nothing you have to purchase.

Buying your own is optional

Some families prefer their student have a copy that stays home and can be written in. If that's you, it's about $15 from Amazon or any music retailer. Get the edition for your student's specific instrument.

Lessons

Not required, but this is where students separate

Private lessons aren't required for Upper School band. I'll be direct: the students carrying the strongest parts are almost always the ones taking them. At this level a teacher is the difference between holding a chair and leading a section.

Practice

90 minutes a week, same as every grade

The practice standard doesn't loosen as students get older. Ninety minutes a week, minimum. Three sessions of 30 minutes is what I recommend, but any split that reaches 90 counts the same. In a performing ensemble it matters more, not less. One unprepared part is audible.

Changed: the practice log form is now the only place minutes count. MakeMusic Cloud is still in use as a place for your student to practice and to submit scales assignments, but I am no longer measuring practice time through it, because the way it counts minutes is too restrictive. Submit every session on the practice log form.

What counts as practice

Yes: your student playing on their own, at home.

No: band class or rehearsal. That is ensemble time, not practice.

No: time in a private lesson. That is instruction, not practice.

Practicing after a lesson, working on what the teacher assigned, absolutely counts. Sitting in the lesson does not.

How a practice week gets counted

A week runs Monday through Sunday. Log by Sunday night.

90 minutes a week, split however you like. Three sessions of 30 minutes is what I recommend, but two longer sessions or seven short ones count exactly the same. The weekly total is the standard.

The practice log form is the only place minutes count. Practice Monday, submit Monday, done. No waiting until you have three sessions to report.

Practicing in MakeMusic still gets logged on the form. The app is where your student practices and submits scales assignments. It does not report minutes to me.

A PDF still counts, but please go digital if you can. The form is the only version I can see without entering anything by hand. If it will not work, the PDF is linked right under the gold button above: fill it in and email it to me. Printing it and sending in a physical sheet is the very last resort.

I review logs weekly. If 10 days pass with nothing logged, you will get a short email from me. That is a heads-up, not a warning.

Two tools, two jobs. MakeMusic Cloud is where your student practices with feedback and submits scales assignments. The practice log form is where minutes get counted, one submission per session, every session, wherever it happened.

Grading

Half the grade is showing up ready. Half is practicing.

Upper School band earns a letter grade, and it is built from two things your student fully controls. Both are already being tracked, so nothing here asks anything new of you.

50% — Ready for class

Three things, every class: on time, instrument in hand, book in hand. One point each. Band meets Tuesday and Thursday, so that is 8 classes and 24 points in a grading cycle.

50% — Practice

Ninety minutes of practice a week, split however your student likes. Three sessions of 30 minutes is what I recommend. Over a four-week cycle that is six hours, submitted one session at a time on the practice log form.

Why half and half. A student who never misses a class but never opens the case at home earns exactly 50. A student who practices faithfully but leaves the instrument at home earns the same. Neither half can carry the grade, and that is deliberate. Band is the one subject where the work between classes is the subject.

The Grading Cycle

Grades post every four weeks, two per quarter, so the first reads like a midterm and the second like a final. You will never be surprised in October by something that started going wrong in August.

90 and above is an A · 80 to 89 a B · 70 to 79 a C · 60 to 69 a D · below 60 an F. Practicing more than 12 times does not add points, but it protects you against a week that gets away from you.

When A Concert Falls In The Quarter

The two halves above are how a quarter without a concert works. In a quarter with a concert, the concert carries half the grade and the other two drop to a quarter each. The weights follow the concert, not the calendar.

One night carries the same weight as eight weeks of showing up, because the concert is the thing all of it was for.

Missed for an emergency

Medical or family emergency, and we arrange a short playing assessment in MakeMusic instead. Full credit is still on the table. Email me as soon as you know.

Missed for anything else

The concert scores zero and the quarter caps at 50. There is no make-up for choosing not to come.

Life Happens

These are written down on purpose, so you never have to wonder and I never have to decide in the moment.

  • Excused absence. Those points come out of the total, not off the grade. Missing class for a real reason cannot hurt your student.
  • Instrument in the shop. Let me know ahead of time and both preparedness and practice are excused for those days.
  • Borrowing a classmate's book counts as not having one. The habit is what is being graded, not the object.
  • The practice log form is down. A handwritten log is accepted that week, no penalty.
  • Joining band mid-cycle. Grading starts at your student's first class, not before it.
  • Two unprepared days in a row gets you a note from me in Veracross the same day. Three in a row and I call you. I would rather have one real conversation than send you three notices.
